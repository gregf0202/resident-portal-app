# NaloHub — Feature Register

*The running record of what NaloHub does, what's just been built, and what's on the
horizon. Written for people, not developers — one line per feature, no jargon.*

**Last updated: 22 September 2026** · Maintained at every stage of development.

## 1 · Core features — the foundation

Everything a building gets on day one. Listed in the order it was built.

**Getting in & getting around**
- **Two ways in, no password** — residents tap a link in their sign-in email, or type the code from the same email. The code works on any device, so reading the email on one screen and signing in on another is no longer a dead end.
- **Dashboard** — the building at a glance: weather, quick actions, what's on, and guides.
- **Guided tour & first-run guides** — role-tailored walkthroughs for committee, owners and tenants, which retire themselves once done.
- **Themes & building branding** — each building picks its look, logo and even renames menu sections to suit itself.
- **Add to home screen** — the portal behaves like an app on any phone, with instructions that describe what the person sees on their own phone rather than an iOS version.

**Everyday resident life**
- **Announcements** — building notices, pinnable, with expiry dates, formal notice types (e.g. Notice of AGM), targeting to all residents / owners / specific people — and real email to residents alongside the in-app notice.
- **Alerts** — every application, vote and issue lands in one feed the moment it happens.
- **Maintenance reporting** — snap it, send it, and watch the repair progress (Reported → Seen → Underway → Done).
- **Applications & Bookings** — BBQ, visitor parking and shared spaces; plus pet, renovation and parking applications with printable permits.
- **Events** — building social calendar with RSVPs.
- **Gallery** — the building's shared photo wall.
- **Marketplace** — neighbour-to-neighbour buy/sell.
- **Messaging** — residents message the committee or manager without hunting for an email address.
- **Directory** — opt-in resident directory, with privacy switches each person controls.
- **Business Directory** — trusted local trades and services, recommended by neighbours.
- **Documents** — by-laws, insurance, minutes and budgets, findable in seconds with visibility controls.
- **Fire Safety** — emergency contacts, safety notes and the building's evacuation plan, front and centre.
- **WhatsApp link** — one tap from the portal into the building's existing group chat.

**Committee engine room**
- **Unit Search** — the 30-second unit health check: owners, tenants, pets, vehicles, keys and history.
- **Voting** — motions decided on phones with comments, automatic majorities, an audit trail, and signable proxy forms.
- **Meetings** — agendas, minutes, motions and attendance in one place.
- **Maintenance Workflow** — a repair run end-to-end: triage → quotes → recommendation → vote → contractor confirmed.
- **Approvals** — new residents and requests reviewed and decided in-app.
- **Action Register** — committee to-dos with owners, due dates and overdue flags.
- **Reports** — the numbers on how the building is running.
- **Registers** — Contracts, Contractors, Asset Register and Key & Fob Register, with expiry warnings before anything renews unnoticed.
- **Walk-Through** — the monthly inspection: tick the checklist, snap photos, export a Word report with evidence.
- **Correspondence Hub** — the building's own two-way email record with strata, insurers, solicitors, council and contractors, on one clean building address (e.g. seahaven@send.nalohub.com). Committee-only, append-only, tamper-evident, with an Unfiled tray for strays.
- **Compliance Calendar** *(premium)* — statutory deadlines with traffic lights, so nothing slips past quietly.
- **Dispute Records** *(premium)* — a tamper-evident, append-only record of complaints and their handling.
- **NaloPilot** *(premium)* — ask questions of strata legislation in plain English.
- **By-Laws** *(premium)* — the building's own rules, searchable.
- **Billing** — the building's plan and invoices, with card payment set-up.
- **Data export** — one tap exports everything the building owns; the building's records belong to the building.
- **Help hub** — themed plain-English Q&A with search, for when anyone gets stuck.

## 2 · Built in the last four weeks *(mid August → 22 September 2026)*
- **Correct a finding, with the correction on the record** *(22 September, v0.36.0)*: a finding raised with the wrong owner, date or class could not be fixed on screen, and underneath, anyone who could raise findings could change them with no trace at all. Now the committee can amend any open finding, and has to say why. The change and the reason go into the finding's history for anyone to see, what it said before and what it says now, and the database refuses a change that comes without a reason. Nothing already recorded is ever overwritten.
- **A job done between walks gets its photo, and its credit** *(22 September, v0.36.0)*: work finished between walks could be closed, but without an after photo, and it then fell out of every report. The finding now has its own camera, and the report counts a closure between walks against the next walk, with its before-and-after pair. The most recent walk's report also lists anything closed since, so a copy pulled for a meeting is current to the minute.
- **One tap for a clean area, one tap for an area not walked** *(22 September, v0.36.0)*: every duty group on a walk now has Nothing to report, which answers all its questions OK at once, and Not walked, which records the group and the reason on the walk. A group that was not walked is listed as such in the report and is no longer counted as inspected. On a clean month, 176 questions become thirteen taps.
- **The report opens straight away** *(22 September, v0.36.0)*: a report full of photos takes a while to build, and Safari was treating the late-opening tab as a pop-up and blocking it. The tab now opens the moment Export PDF is pressed, says it is working on it and counts the photos, then becomes the report.
- **Search every finding, including closed ones** *(22 September, v0.36.0)*: one box finds any finding by its reference, where it is or what it says, open or closed, and a Closed tile lists what has been verified done. A closed finding is a fixed record: it cannot be reopened or changed, and a problem that comes back is raised as a new finding that names the old one.
- **The billing password is out of the code** *(22 September, v0.36.0)*: the daily billing run and the inbound dispute email were protected by a password written into their code and into the web address that calls them. Both now live only in the database's secure vault, were replaced with new random values nobody has seen, and travel out of sight in a header. The old ones no longer work.
- **Proxies count from the right day** *(22 September, v0.36.0)*: a proxy vote was checked against the date in Greenwich, so a proxy starting today could not be used before 10am and one that ended yesterday could still be used until 10am. It now follows the local calendar, as does the overdue flag on walk-through findings.
- **Today means today, everywhere** *(22 September, v0.35.4)*: the walk-through date fault turned out to be a pattern. Anywhere NaloHub worked out a date, from the database, the servers or the app, it was using the calendar in Greenwich, and a Queensland morning is still yesterday there until 10am. So invoice dates, parking permit approval dates, proxy start dates, the date on a proxy form, decisions, notifications, key receipts, dispute and correspondence dates, move-out dates and the AGM pack's prepared-on date could all read a day early. The billing run, which happens at 6am, had been running every day against the previous day, so automatic charges were a day late. All of it now uses the local calendar, from one source in the database and one helper in the app. No building was billed wrongly: every billed building is still in its trial.
- **A walk dated the day it happened** *(22 September, v0.35.3)*: a walk started before 10am was being dated the day before, because the database kept its calendar in UTC, and a Queensland morning is still yesterday in UTC. The walk opened at 9:31 on 20 September was filed as 19 September. For an inspection record that is not a cosmetic error: the date is the first thing anyone checks. Walks and findings now take the date from the phone they are recorded on, with the Brisbane date as the fallback, so the early October walks will carry the right day. The same fault turned out to be elsewhere too; see v0.35.4 above.
- **Every walk ends issued** *(22 September, v0.35.3)*: the walk screen had two ways to finish, and only one of them worked. Complete walk, the bright button at the top, ended the walk without issuing it, and a walk ended that way could never be reopened, never counted towards how the building is tracking, and printed as a draft forever. It is now Go to Finish, which takes you to the one place a walk is finished properly, and any walk that was never issued can be continued and issued. The in-app walk-through guide was rewritten for the register at the same time; it still told people to press Complete walk.
- **A year of decisions, tabled without retyping** *(20 September, v0.35.2)*: All requests in Applications & Bookings can now be narrowed by search (unit, name, rego, words), by type, by status with live counts, and — for the committee — by how the decision was made (BCC vote or direct approval) and when it was submitted, defaulting to the last twelve months. "Export for AGM pack" writes whatever is on screen as a branded Word table, one row per request with its conditions of approval beneath it, so the committee can table the year's applications and bookings at the AGM from the record rather than from memory.
- **Scan the permit, see if it is current** *(20 September, v0.35.1)*: a parking permit on a dash now carries a QR code that anyone in the car park can scan. It opens a small page on portal.nalohub.com showing the permit number, the building, the dates it covers and whether it is current today, checked live against the building's record. It shows nothing about who the resident is or what the car is; that is on the printed permit, not the scan. Behind it is a new public `permit-verify` service that answers by the permit's hidden id, so a number cannot be guessed. Alongside it, the permit itself has been redesigned as an A4 sheet that folds into a tent for the dash: navy header, the building's name front and centre, the NaloHub mark, the QR, and an aerial photo of the building. The permit the app prints is that design from today: every parking permit issued now comes out as the tent, with the QR already on it. If it has expired, the print says so across the face.
- **The walk-through stops forgetting what it found last month** *(20 September, v0.35.0)*: a walk-through could record what it saw, and that was all. Every issue lived as a line of free text inside one walk, so the next walk started from nothing. Curve's August and September walkaround reports carry the same items over and over with no way to show it, which is exactly the thing a body corporate needs to be able to show. NaloHub now keeps a permanent findings register alongside the walk. An issue is raised once, gets a reference (CB-0001), and every later sighting, photo and closure attaches to that same finding. "Third time this has been raised" is now a fact on the record rather than somebody's recollection.
- **The checklist is Curve's own agreement, question by question** *(20 September, v0.35.0)*: the old walk-through asked 22 generic questions. It now asks 176, in thirteen sections, and every one of them names the line of Schedule 1 of the Caretaking and Letting Agreement it tests and the frequency that line contracts for. Twenty-four questions rest only on the general clauses and say so plainly, rather than implying a duty that is not written down. One thing the exercise turned up: the sauna has no duty line anywhere in the agreement. The old twenty-two questions are retired, not deleted, so past walks still read correctly.
- **Six kinds of finding, and standard shortfalls stay out of the repair log** *(20 September, v0.35.0)*: not every observation is a repair. A finding is now classed as a standard shortfall, a rectification, contracted works, a hazard, a lot owner or by-law matter, or governance. Hazards must carry a risk rating before they can be saved. Standard shortfalls are blocked by the database from ever entering the Maintenance Workflow, which sounds like a technicality and is not: the average-days-to-resolve and open-now figures are the building's answer to "prove it", and filling them with cleaning misses would ruin the one number that matters.
- **Photo evidence, before and after, in a report you can actually email** *(20 September, v0.35.0)*: findings pair the photo from when the issue was raised with the photo from when it was closed. Reports carry those pairs as small thumbnails, around 15 KB each, while the full-size images stay in the app, so a report with sixty photographs in it still sends. Both the PDF and the Word export now carry the same design, the NaloHub logo, and a footer on every page with the building, the walk date and the moment the copy was generated, so nobody in a meeting is quietly working off last week's printout.
- **Closing an item without pretending to walk the building** *(20 September, v0.35.0)*: work gets finished between walks, and the only way to record that was to open a walk that never happened. Findings can now be marked still present, or confirmed done and closed, from the register itself, and the record says which: "recorded against the register, not a walk". Closure still needs committee verification, enforced by the database. The walk screen also stops offering to start a second walk when one is already open, which is how a duplicate appeared on 19 September.

- **The key register learns what your building actually calls its keys** *(19 September, v0.34.0)*: the committee asked for thirteen descriptors, from "Building Key - Master" through seven fire stairs levels to "Unit door metal lock key". Rather than hard-code that list, NaloHub now lets each building write its own. Curve's thirteen are loaded exactly as the committee worded them, and when an eighth level or a second tower appears, someone types a line rather than waiting for a software release. A building down the road with twelve levels is not stuck with Curve's seven.
- **Entitlement, issued, and on hand** *(19 September, v0.34.0)*: every unit can now carry a set entitlement for each kind of key, fob or remote, and the register tracks which of those are actually issued and which are sitting with the building manager. That is the difference between a list of keys and a record you can reconcile. Entitlements can be typed in one unit at a time or loaded for the whole building from the committee's own spreadsheet.
- **Signed for, by name, with the paperwork attached** *(19 September, v0.34.0)*: an issue now records who physically took the device, whether they are the owner, the managing agent or the tenant, and the signed receipt is uploaded and kept with it. Issuing to a tenant asks for the owner's authority first, because that is the building's rule. Keys are still issued to the unit; the person is part of the issuance record, not a replacement for it.
- **Lost does not mean gone** *(19 September, v0.34.0)*: a lost or withdrawn fob or remote is suspended, never deleted, and stays on the record against the unit or against stock. A key register that forgets things is not a key register.
- **The annual audit, as one page** *(19 September, v0.34.0)*: the Caretaker's yearly check now runs from a single reconciliation. Every unit and descriptor with its entitlement, what is issued, what is on hand and any discrepancy, plus the building's own stock of fobs and remotes to count against, and a download to sign off. Anything not yet identified is listed rather than left out, so the report never looks tidier than the underlying records are.
- **Parking permits print the unit number** *(18 September, v0.33.2)*: an approved parking permit was producing its printable dash permit with the unit line blank. The permit was taking the unit from the building's unit register rather than from the application itself, and most buildings have no register loaded yet, so there was nothing to take — both permits issued so far came out that way. A permit on a windscreen exists to connect a car to a lot, so that was the one line which could not be empty. The application form now asks for the unit and will not send without it, and the permit prints what the resident typed. Where a building does have its register loaded the permit also attaches itself to that unit's record, so it appears in the unit history alongside the pets, keys and vehicles; where it does not, the resident still gets a working permit rather than being told their unit does not exist.
- **A way back that is actually where you are looking** *(18 September, v0.33.1)*: every screen had a back button, tucked into the coloured banner at the very top. On a long screen, and a correspondence thread is the longest in NaloHub, you had scrolled well past it by the time you wanted it, leaving no obvious way out except the browser. Back now sits in the body of the page alongside the main action, on every screen that has somewhere to go back to, and a thread carries a second one at the foot. The rule NaloHub already followed for main actions, that a control people need should be where the content is rather than in the decoration, now covers going back too.
- **The party list knows about tenants and building managers** *(18 September, v0.33.1)*: when recording who a piece of correspondence is with, the two parties a committee deals with most after the owner, the tenant living in the lot and the building manager, were not on the list at all. Everyone was being filed as "Other", which is where all the existing contacts had ended up. Both are now there. "Agent" has become "Managing agent", because in strata "agent" could mean three different people. The list is also reordered by who you actually deal with most often, rather than the order the options happened to be written in years ago.
- **The email that had nowhere to go** *(18 September, v0.33.0)*: an email arrived at Curve's building address, appeared in the Unfiled tray exactly as intended, and then could not be filed. The only option was to add it to an existing conversation, and Curve had none, so the File button sat greyed out with no explanation. Behind that was a circle: filing needed a conversation, and the only way to start a conversation was to send an email. Every building would have hit it on its very first incoming email. Now an email can start its own conversation. Tap "File as a new thread", confirm who it is from and what to call the thread, and the email becomes the first entry in a permanent record. Nothing is sent, and nobody is contacted. The person is added to the building's contacts automatically, and filing a second email from the same address reuses them rather than creating a duplicate.
- **Search that reads the emails, not just the labels** *(18 September, v0.33.0)*: one box now searches the subject, the name, email address and organisation of the party, and the words inside every message on record. Looking for the exchange where someone mentioned the lift motor finds it even if the subject says nothing about lifts, and the matching sentence is shown with the words picked out. Committee memory is usually a phrase, not a filing reference, so that is what it searches on. The Unfiled tray gained its own filter for when a backlog has built up.
- **Notices now come from your building, not from "no reply"** *(18 September, v0.33.0)*: announcements were being sent from a no-reply address, while the footer of the same email invited people to reply. Anyone who looked at the header learned not to bother. Worse, hitting Reply All sent the reply to two of our addresses at once, so the same message arrived twice and one copy could not be matched to any building and vanished from view, and every notice was quietly delivered back to us as well. Notices now come from the building's own address, so Reply and Reply All both land in one place, the building's record. Six stray copies from the old behaviour were identified and set aside, and no resident email was lost.
- **A record that refuses to be rewritten, proven by accident** *(18 September)*: while testing the new filing, an attempt to undo a test entry was refused by the database itself. Correspondence is append-only by design: a filed email cannot be quietly deleted or edited, by anyone, including us. It is easy to claim that in a sales sheet. It is better to find out it is true when you try to tidy something up and the record will not let you.
- **The key register that could not see the keys** *(18 September, v0.32.0)*: Curve Birtinya's key and fob data uploaded successfully in August. 230 keys, across 54 apartments, every one with its key number recorded. The Key & Fob Register showed almost none of it. Both of those things were true at once, because there were two key registers inside NaloHub that had never been introduced to each other. The register screen was reading an older store, while the upload, and every key ever added through Unit Search, went to the newer one. Nothing was lost and nothing imported badly. The screen was looking in the wrong drawer. It now reads the same records as Unit Search, so all 230 keys are there, and anything added in either place shows up in both.
- **One box, three ways to search** *(18 September, v0.32.0)*: type an apartment number, a resident's name or a key number into a single search box and the list narrows. Chips filter by issued, returned, lost or deactivated, and two menus filter by what a device is and what it is for. A committee member asked "who has a key to 204" no longer needs to know which of three things to search by.
- **Keys, fobs and cards, master and service** *(18 September, v0.32.0)*: the register already knew what a device was, a key, a fob, a card, a remote. It could not record what a device was for. Those are two different questions, and a master fob is the proof: it is a fob, and it is a master. So rather than one longer list where a master fob has to pretend to be one or the other, there are now two fields. Type says what it is. Purpose says whether it is a resident's, a master, a service key or something else. Every existing key stayed a resident key, which is what it was.
- **Building keys finally have somewhere to live** *(18 September, v0.32.0)*: master keys, service keys and the emergency access lock box belong to the building, not to any one apartment. Until now the register insisted every device be filed against an apartment, which is why the August upload had to skip the building manager's set, the fire warden key set and the lock box entirely. Those can now be recorded properly, and they sort to the top of the register, where the committee wants them.
- **The register admits what it does not know** *(18 September, v0.32.0)*: none of Curve's 230 keys has a person's name against it, because the building manager's register recorded each key against the apartment rather than the holder. That is normal, and it is not something software should paper over. Each of those keys reads "Holder not recorded", in amber, with the apartment's current residents shown underneath and clearly labelled as residents rather than holders. A tile at the top counts how many devices are in that state, so the size of the gap is visible instead of buried. Guessing would have been easy and would have been worse: a key register is the kind of record that gets quoted in a dispute, and a name that was inferred rather than recorded is a liability the moment anyone relies on it.
- **Taking the register with you** *(18 September, v0.32.0)*: the whole register, or whatever the current search and filters are showing, downloads as a spreadsheet in one tap. Uploading a register works the same way as before, with the building manager's own key numbers, and now understands purpose as well.

- **The Share button that was not there** *(5 September, v0.31.3)* — the Add to Home Screen instructions said "tap Share". On a current iPhone in Safari's default Compact layout there is no Share button next to the address bar, only a "..." button, and Share is inside it. Curve's Chairperson followed the instruction, could not find the button, and stopped. Both the floating Add to Home Screen panel and the Getting Started step now describe what the person will see rather than what version of iOS they have: if you see the Share icon (a box with an arrow), tap it; if you see "...", tap that, then Share. Then Add to Home Screen, then Add. Both icons are drawn in the panel so there is nothing to interpret. Android was already one route (the ⋮ menu, or Chrome's own Install prompt) and is unchanged. The lesson generalises: a phone instruction that names a button the person may not have is a stumble, and a stumble at the home screen step is where committee members quietly give up.
- **The update banner reaches production** *(v0.31.2)* — the "there is a newer version, tap Refresh" banner shipped in v0.30.0 had only ever run on the demo site. It was built inside the demo shell and never mounted in the production app, so the one thing it existed to fix (people sitting on an old build) was still happening on real phones. Caught when an iPhone home-screen install sat on v0.30.0 with no prompt while a laptop and an Android phone had both moved on. Two lines: export the banner, mount it in production. Every installed app now gets the prompt.
- **Printable guides stop saying the name twice** *(v0.31.1)* — all twenty printable cheat sheets carried the NaloHub logo and the word "NaloHub" beside it, because the logo already is the wordmark. The text is now hidden and only appears if the hosted logo fails to load, so a printout is branded either way. The hosted logo was also replaced with a transparent version; the old one printed a grey rectangle behind the mark on white paper.
- **Two ways into NaloHub** *(4 September, v0.31.0)* — first sign-in is the gate everything else sits behind: a committee member who fails twice does not try a third time, they wait for the next meeting and say they could not get in. The link alone had three quiet failure modes that look identical to the person and are invisible to us. They open the email on the laptop and the phone stays locked out, because each device holds its own sign-in. A workplace mail scanner opens the link on their behalf to check it is safe, and because the link works once, it is spent before they tap it. Or the link opens inside the mail app's own browser, where "Add to Home Screen" is not offered. The sign-in email now carries a code as well, and the waiting screen accepts it. The code is just digits, so it survives all three: read it off whichever screen the email is on and type it into the device you actually want to use. The link stays as the one-tap path; nothing was taken away. The waiting screen also names the sender and points at Junk and Outlook's "Other" tab, which is where a first email to a Microsoft address usually lands, and the one-a-minute limit on resending is now explained as a pause rather than shown as an error. Prompted by Curve's Chairperson, who could reach NaloHub on her laptop but not her phone.
- **Knowing who actually uses NaloHub** *(3 to 4 September, database then app, v0.31.0)* — the platform records one line per person, per building, per day, when they open their building. Opens rather than sign-ins, because a sign-in lasts weeks and one login can hide a month of daily use, or none. This is the number that says where onboarding help is needed and whether a building is thriving rather than merely launched. Two reports ride on it, weekly by default and any date range on request: presence (how many people were active, per building, per role) and actions (what was actually done, with settings autosave noise stripped out). Privacy is deliberate: committees never see individual login times, only the platform does. Internal test buildings are flagged and excluded so the numbers stay honest. Each row also carries a coarse phone-or-computer marker, which gives the list that matters most right now: people who have only ever opened their building on a desktop, before they think to complain.
- **A live credential closed off** *(4 September, database only, no app release)* — a routine security scan found a database function that returned an internal secret and could be called by anyone, signed in or not, using the key that ships inside the app's own code and is public by design. Tested rather than assumed: it returned the secret. Access is now revoked from everyone except the server itself, and re-tested as an anonymous caller, which is refused. Two reporting functions had the same over-broad permission; both already refused to return anything to a non-admin, but the permission is now revoked as well. Nothing about this was visible to residents or committees, and there is no sign it was found by anyone else.

- **Who maintains the unit register** *(30 August, v0.29)* — the building manager is usually the first to know a tenant has moved or a key has changed hands, but could only watch the register go stale. Now each committee decides for itself: one switch in Settings lets their building manager keep owners, tenants, pets, vehicles and keys current. Off by default, committee-controlled, enforced by the database rather than by hiding buttons, and by-law breaches stay committee-only either way, because recording an allegation against a resident is a committee act rather than register upkeep.
- **Versioned Conditions of Approval** *(30 August, v0.29)* — the integrity fix for committee votes. Until now any committee member could add or remove Conditions of Approval on an open motion at any moment, including after four of six had already voted, with nothing recording who changed what: a member could vote yes on five conditions and the motion pass on three. Now conditions are versioned and a vote only ever counts against the version its voter saw. The inline add and remove are gone, replaced by **Amend**: any BCC member, reason mandatory. Saving snapshots the outgoing conditions and every vote cast against them into the motion's history, bumps the version, sets those votes aside (kept on the record, never erased) and re-alerts every BCC member to vote again on what they can now see. The card shows the version, the tally says which version it counts, and an **Amendment history** panel gives each change with a strike-through diff, who, when, why, and every vote set aside with how it was cast. When the motion passes, the winning version binds and the decision names it.
- **Disputes finally reach the unit they concern** *(30 August, v0.29)* — the Disputes section in Unit Search has been decorative since it was built: nothing ever wrote the unit against a dispute, so it could never populate for any unit in any building. Complaints now carry a lot in both directions, logged from the unit or picked from a list when logging a complaint, and a unit's history finally shows what has been raised about it.
- **Nobody gets left on an old version** *(31 August, v0.30.0)* — a quiet problem worth naming: fixing a bug does not mean anyone receives the fix. There is no service worker, so any genuine page load already fetches the newest code, but nothing makes people load the page. A committee member with the app open on their phone all week, or anyone who followed the "Add to Home Screen" guide (iOS gives them no address bar and no pull-to-refresh), could sit on a build from a fortnight ago and never know. Now every build publishes its own version number, and the app checks it on launch, whenever it comes back to the front, and every fifteen minutes. When there is something newer, a small banner says so and offers Refresh. It never reloads by itself, because somebody may be halfway through writing up a repair, and it stays silent when the phone is offline. Repeat visits also got faster: the files that can never change are now cached properly, which matters on mobile data. *(Footnote from v0.31.2: this banner only reached the demo site at first; see above.)*

## 3 · Considered & pending — for a later review

**Near horizon (specified, awaiting build)**


- **Permit photo per building** — the tent permit shows Curve's aerial photo because Curve is the only building with one loaded. A `heroImage` on the building record, uploaded in Settings, would let every building carry its own. Small.

*Monthly-opens trio (agreed 7 September) — the three features chosen to lift monthly opens above 50% of units, which is the number that sells building two. Built in this order, one at a time, each shipped and measured against `activity_events` before the next starts.*
- **Building calendar feed** — one tap subscribes the resident's own phone calendar to the building: events, AGM and meeting dates, bookings they hold, fire drills, lift and water outages, bin days. Works even when they never open the app, which is the point: the building shows up where they already look. Per-building tokenised ICS URL served from the backend; unsubscribing is deleting the calendar, nothing to manage in-app.
- **Quick Polls** — one-tap informal polls with live results and no quorum: "pool heating on through May?", "BBQ area until 10pm?". Deliberately separate from Voting (no audit trail, no motion, no proxies). Any committee member or BM can post one; residents answer in a second from the dashboard. Committee gets a temperature read before drafting a motion, and a one-tap "convert to motion" carries the question into Voting when it is time to decide properly.
- **Neighbour helpers board** — can lend / can help: a ladder, a drill, parcel collection, plant watering while away, pet feeding. Marketplace is buy and sell; this is favours. Offers and asks, opt-in, matched to the Directory's existing privacy switches so nobody is listed who has not chosen to be. Highest goodwill per line of code in the plan.
- **Interest circles** *(queued behind the trio)* — opt-in micro groups (dog walkers, parents, retirees, book club) where any member can "propose a meet", which creates an Event with invites scoped to the circle. Held until the trio is live and the data shows which circles residents actually form.
- **General-purpose bulk import (sheet per table)** — the Curve import was run by hand against the database. Turn it into something a committee or building manager can do themselves: upload a workbook of units, people, vehicles, pets, keys, breaches, agents and notes, preview what will change, then commit. Scoped 1 to 2 days. The Curve staging workbook is the working spec.
- **Bulk by-law upload** — today by-laws can only be added one at a time, typed or pasted, which is not a job a volunteer committee will ever finish. This replaces that: upload the document once, we pull out every by-law, and the committee checks the list and publishes. It's built for scanned documents, because that's what buildings actually have — Curve Birtinya's own by-laws arrived as an 11-page scan with no readable text in it, at 5.1 MB, which is over the app's upload limit; extracted, the same 38 by-laws are 46 KB. Three ways in: upload it; ask your strata manager (the wording written for you, sent from Correspondence so the reply lands on the record); or send it to us and we'll load it, with the committee still doing the final approval. Nothing reaches residents or NaloPilot until the committee approves it, and the approval is stamped with who and when, so the by-laws page can honestly say *"current as at"*. The single-by-law form stays, for the amendment passed at an AGM. Worked mock and full build spec exist (`bylaws-upload-mockup.html` and `BYLAWS_INGEST_PROCESS.md`, 8 Aug). **The NaloPilot half of this shipped on 8 August** — Ask Nalo now answers from a building's own by-laws, scoped so residents only ever see their own building's rules — and Curve Birtinya's 38 by-laws are loaded and live as the first real set; what remains to build is the committee-facing upload, review and approval flow.
- **Recognition Phase 2** — activity badges (Loop Closer, Good Neighbour), reactions on notices, "seen by X residents" social proof, and a committee adoption pulse showing the launch working.
- **Suggestion loop** — "Suggest something" with a visible Suggested → Noted → Considered → Actioned trail, and "You asked, we did" announcements. The committee's one promise: answer everything.
- **Recognition Phase 3** — learning badges, resident anniversaries, richer belonging touches.
- **Second-tier guides** — proxy appointments remains the one deferred guide (Correspondence was promoted and built in v0.24). Sections have replaced the flat-list ceiling: the rule is now ~8 per section-view.
